Young people make up a large share of the voting-age population in Latin America, yet they hold only a small fraction of the seats in national parliaments. This gap matters: institutions that do not reflect the generation that will live with their decisions lose legitimacy over time.

## Three obstacles

1. **Candidacy rules.** In several countries the minimum age to run for office is higher than the voting age.
2. **Party structures.** Lists are often closed to newcomers, and youth wings have little say in nominations.
3. **Campaign costs.** Without public financing or party support, running is out of reach for most young candidates.

## What parties can do

Generational change must be sponsored actively through party statutes. Youth quotas on electoral lists, training programmes and mentoring by sitting representatives are proven tools.

> "Democracy is not inherited, it is defended."
